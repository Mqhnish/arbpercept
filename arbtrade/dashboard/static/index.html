This site is a static copy of the dashboard, built from the reports committed to the repo (). Every number on it is a replay on development days. Both locked test sets are still unopened, and no real orders were placed.

The problem

The same perpetual future trades on Binance and Bybit, and the two prices drift apart for a moment many times a day. Spotting such a gap is easy. By the time our orders reach both venues, though, the gap has often closed. Binance is 50 ms away from our machine and Bybit 8 ms, and the taker fees are 1.7 and 3.0 bps a leg, so most gaps are gone or too small by the time we could act. The question is which gaps will still be there when the orders land, and whether that is worth the fees.

How the system decides

Each gap is scored at the latency it will face before the orders arrive:

S(τ) = q(τ)·(ê − c) + (1 − q(τ))·ℓ̄(τ)

q is the probability that both immediate-or-cancel legs fill at the prices we saw, after latency τ. ê is the gap measured against the venues' usual offset rather than against zero, c is both legs' fees, and ℓ̄ is the average outcome when a trade does not fully fill, measured on training days. A cheap logistic prior (1.5 µs) scores every gap. When it is unsure, a gradient-boosted model with 23 features (about 15 µs) decides. That is the "cascade".

What the replays show

Every policy is replayed on the same days with per-venue latency and fees. Fills use up the displayed size, and each trade's position, funding and closing cost are tracked under a $50k cap. Validation days come from walk-forward folds, and the intervals are day-bootstrap 95% intervals.

On the 100 ms archive (Binance ticks, Bybit snapshots every 100 ms) the cascade makes $15 a day on BTC (95% CI $7 to $23, 205 days) and $15 on ETH ($11 to $21) with optimistic fills. With conservative fills it breaks even there. The archive sees Bybit only in 100 ms snapshots, and the conservative bound fills a leg only when the snapshots on both sides of its arrival allow it.

Trading every gap loses $94 to $925 a day on every coin. When the decay model picks the trades, the cascade's interval is above zero on five of the nine coins, and it beats the gap-size rule on five.

Tardis.dev tick data (the free first day of each month, 2020 to 2026) records every quote change on both venues. There the conservative fills keep $10 a day on BTC, $12 on ETH and $10 on SOL, each interval above zero. Pairs with OKX show no edge at our assumed OKX fee and latency.

Will the gap last?

Gap size alone barely predicts whether a gap survives (AUC 0.49 to 0.58 on the cross-venue and basis pairs); the decay model reaches 0.78 to 0.84 on the archive. On our own 10 ms recordings it scores 0.64 to 0.74 at 50 to 500 ms, so the archive figures are an upper bound.

Paper trader

runs on a local checkout

The paper trader connects to Binance and Bybit's live order books, watches the gap against their usual offset, and simulates both legs of every trade it would take. No orders are sent. It needs a live connection to the exchanges, so this static copy cannot run it. To try it, start the dashboard locally (python -m arbtrade.dashboard --open, see Overview) and open this tab.
